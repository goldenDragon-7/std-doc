Freeze & ship

turn the conversation into a thing you can hand to anyone

While you're working, a living doc is alive — the 💬 panel listens, comments flow in and replies flow out. Freezing is the moment it's done and you want to send it.
It bakes the whole page into one self-contained file with nothing left to phone home — no server, no internet, no dependencies. A frozen doc opens by double-click, forever, on a laptop in a cave.

❄ when you ship

FROZEN
  • one HTML file — every asset inlined, every diagram baked to SVG
  • no server, no network, no dependency; opens over file://
  • the comment widget is stripped — a finished artifact, not a draft
  • travels as a file or a .zip; identical on every machine
Freeze means frozen.
The security gate refuses to ship a “frozen” doc that secretly still reaches out — no external script, no CDN font, no remote image. If a diagram can't be baked to inline SVG, freeze fails loudly rather than shipping something that breaks in the cave. A gift you give away has to work in the recipient's hands, not just yours.
one command bakes the portable snapshot
stddoc freeze source.json published/ --zip

The thing you version and hand off is the JSON, not the rendered page. JSON is the compact, precise form a mind thinks in natively; the HTML is the expressive form. We derive page-from-JSON because that direction is the easy one — expanding a spec is computable; squeezing a page back isn't. Neither is 'truth,' neither is decoration.

freeze is the other half of that — Versioning keeps the compact source; freezing bakes the expressive snapshot. Together they mean the doc is both diffable and giftable — small enough to track, whole enough to send.

Why freezing is the same thesis
The cheapest doc to receive is one that asks nothing of the receiver — no install, no account, no trust in a server staying up. Freeze collapses “here's how to run my doc” into “here's my doc.” Fewer steps, fewer failures, fewer questions — for the person on the other end, who never has to meet your toolchain to read what you made.
