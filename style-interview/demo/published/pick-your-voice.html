Pick your voice (and a name for me)

three Voices, not three skins — and the voice even suggests the name you'd call me

Each voice is a consistent document that rhymes top to bottom — its subject, its diagram, its buttons, the roundness of its corners.
That total self-consistency is “fractal voice,” and it's the cheap path on both ends: the reader spends nothing reconciling mismatches, and the writer chooses once — after that, the voice answers every choice (which diagram, which hue, which corner), so everything flows. Choose once, generate free. And the voice goes all the way down: pick one, and it even suggests the name you'd call me.

why the voice goes all the way down

A voice isn't a coat of paint over fixed content — it's the one decision the whole document descends from. Pick parchment and the page doesn't just change color; it changes what it's about, how it argues, the shape of its diagrams. That's why it can hand you a name: the voice is coherent enough to introduce itself.

The payoff is the same word, every time.
Fewer tokens, fewer errors, fewer questions. Choose once, and the voice suggests every later choice — down to the name you'd call me.
